    How to Use the Star Battle Solver

    The Puzzle

    • The 10 × 10 board is split into 10 colored regions. Place stars so that every row, every column and every region has exactly 2 stars, and no two stars touch, not even corner to corner.

    Getting a Puzzle

    • Examples loads one of four ready-made puzzles, one at each difficulty.
    • Random Puzzle makes a brand-new puzzle at the difficulty you pick.
    • Paint your own: pick a color (A–J), then tap or drag on the board. The eraser, or a right-click, clears cells. Every cell needs a color, and a puzzle needs exactly 10 regions.
    • Save… downloads the puzzle as a small text file; Open… loads one back (the same files the Python version uses).

    Watching It Solve

    • Solve plays the solution step by step (press again to pause); One Step goes one step at a time; Skip to the End jumps to the answer; Reset clears the stars. Every step is explained in the list.
    • The solver always tries the simplest reasoning first and only moves on when it's stuck. Rules 1–4 use the names from Carson's instruction manual for the Star Battles round of the 2022 Berkeley mini Math Tournament:
      1. Counting. Completed Sections: a row, column or region that already has its stars is crossed out; one with exactly enough open cells left gets stars in all of them.
      2. No More Space and Too Much Space: if 3 regions fit inside 3 rows, they use up all those rows' stars; if 3 rows touch only 3 regions, those rows fill them up.
      3. Rude Roommates and Nosy Neighbors: a star here would leave its own row, column or region (or a nearby one) without room for its stars, so it can't go here.
      4. Chunking: any 2×2 square holds at most one star, which limits how many stars two neighboring rows or columns can take.
      5. Deep what-if: pretend there's a star here and follow rules 1 and 2 forward; if that leads to a dead end, cross the cell out.
    • Cells crossed out for the same reason are shown as one step, and the row, column or region the reason is about is tinted.
    • When all five rules are stuck, it guesses (gold box), and undoes the guess if it leads to a dead end (red box).

    Difficulty

    • A puzzle's level is the widest reasoning it needs, meaning how much of the board you have to keep in mind at once. The solver always tries the simplest reasoning first, so the hardest rule it ever uses is the hardest one the puzzle needs.
    • Easy: one or two rows, columns or regions at a time (Completed Sections, Rude Roommates, Nosy Neighbors, a squeeze inside one row). Medium: two or more lines together (chunking, or a squeeze across several rows). Hard: a chain of what-ifs, but no guessing. Expert: logic alone gets stuck, so the solver has to guess. The level shown beside “Watch It Solve” comes from solving the puzzle this way.
